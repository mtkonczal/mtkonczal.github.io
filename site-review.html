Design and interface review

The craft is fine.
The site is pointed at the wrong job.

Reviewed live at mikekonczal.com on desktop (1280 and 1440) and mobile (375), against styles.css, index.qmd, the includes, and the built payload in docs/. Every number below was measured, not estimated.

Verdict

The site has two jobs. It does one of them well and the other almost not at all, and the one it neglects is the one you wrote a goal for.

Job one: credibility

Working

A producer, editor, Hill staffer, or funder googles you and needs to confirm you are real and serious. Bio, credentials, book, 346 press hits, testimony, resume. This is better built than almost any economist's personal site.

Job two: audience growth

Not working

One post a week, 3,000 to 9,000 subscribers. The site offers one sidebar button, no inline capture, and none of your newsletter writing anywhere in the record.

The specific gap

You published on Aug 13, Jul 9, Jun 27, and Jun 23. None of those appear anywhere on this site. Latest tops out at Jun 10. Now says June. Portfolio's Writing tab (66 items) jumps from Feb 2026 to Mar 2025 to 2023, because your most active channel is absent from the record entirely.

A visitor arriving from a media hit sees a site that looks last tended in June, sitting next to a live data strip showing July numbers. The freshest thing on the page is a number. The freshest thing you wrote is invisible.

So the recommendation is not a redesign. The design work here is careful and mostly right. It is a funnel and freshness problem wearing a design problem's clothes.

What works

Worth naming, because the instinct will be to change too much.

  • "Start Here" is the best thing on the site. First person, five pieces, each with a sentence on why it matters and where it sits in your arc. That is exactly what a curious reader needs and almost nobody builds it.
  • The macro strip is a genuine differentiator. Eight indicators rendered from FRED at build time, with a fail-loud and cache-fallback policy in the R. No other personal site has this.
  • The book band is the one real visual moment. Dark petrol panel, copper links, cover, four blurbs. It borrows from the jacket instead of inventing a look, which is the right call.
  • Newsreader plus Inter is a real pairing, not the default stack. Both actually load. Verified with document.fonts.check(). Served via the Google Fonts @import at styles.css:7.
  • The token system is documented and deliberate. Named palette with contrast ratios reasoned about in the comments, which is rarer than it should be.
  • The accessibility floor is partly built already. 3 focus-visible rule sets, 1 prefers-reduced-motion block, lang="en", real alt text on all 4 images, aria-label on the carousel buttons.
  • The Projects copy is concrete. Both tidyusmacro and Draftwatch say what the thing does rather than what it aspires to.

Quick wins

Ordered by value per hour. Items 4 through 8 are under an hour combined.

Freshness

  1. Pull the Substack RSS into "Latest" at render time. newsletter.mikekonczal.com/feed. Four missing posts is the whole staleness problem, and this fixes it permanently instead of once.

  2. Kill the "Now" carousel. It is prev/next arrows for two paragraphs of text, and it is the last thing on the page, so the closing impression is "updated June 2026." Keep one entry and move the date next to the section heading, where it reads as a freshness signal rather than a confession.

Newsletter funnel

  1. Add an inline Substack embed right after "Start Here." That is the moment a reader has just been given five reasons to care. Today the only subscribe path is a sidebar button that opens a new tab. This is the highest value thirty minutes on the list.

Metadata

  1. Add description: to the index.qmd front matter. You set it in _quarto.yml at the site level, but Quarto only emits the tag when it is on the page. Google is currently writing your snippet for you.

    Measured on docs/index.html: 0 matches for name="description", 0 for og:description, 0 for rel="canonical".

  2. Make a 1200 x 630 social card. Every share is being center-cropped right now.

    og:image is the hero photo at 1171 x 998 (near square), served under twitter:card: summary_large_image, which wants 1.91:1.

  3. Give the page a real title. Currently just "Mike Konczal." Make it "Mike Konczal | Economist and Writer."

Weight

  1. Resize the four images and add loading="lazy". Saves roughly 1.4 MB of a 2 MB first load. The Draftwatch screenshot alone is about 1 MB of the fix.

    images/draftwatch_image.png is 1.08 MB at 3044 x 1650, displayed at about 300px wide, loaded eagerly, three screens below the fold. Book cover: 920 x 1362 at 274 KB, displayed at about 145px. Hero: 240 KB. No image on the page uses loading="lazy".

  2. Drop the dead search payload. You ship site_libs/quarto-search (156 KB) plus a 54 KB search.json for a feature _quarto.yml explicitly disables, and flatly's Lato webfonts you never use.

Contrast and accessibility

  1. Darken --mk-mute to about #6a7577. One value, and it is the only contrast failure in the palette. This document uses the darkened value so you can see it in context.

    #7e8a8c on #fbfcfc computes to 3.46:1, below the 4.5:1 AA floor for normal text. It is used on the "DATA I'M WATCHING" eyebrow at 12.24px and the DataTable "1–5 of 66" line. #6a7577 gives about 4.6:1.

  2. Restore the H1 to the accessibility tree. #title-block-header { display: none; } hides it from screen readers too, so the homepage has no H1 at all for assistive tech. Use a visually-hidden class, or promote the hero to a real H1 (which you should do anyway, see below).

  3. Add a skip link. There is a fixed eight-item nav rail on every page and no way past it from the keyboard.

  4. Fix the mobile hamburger overlap. At 375px the floating button sits on top of body text, covering the tail of the "Rewriting the Rules of the American Economy" link. Add bottom padding to the content column.

  5. Make the sparkline tooltips work off desktop. They are bound to mousemove only, with no focus or touch handling. On mobile, where the strip is roughly 3,600px of scroll, the entire interactive layer is dead.

    index.qmd lines 355 to 390.

  6. Give the portfolio search input an aria-label. It has a placeholder and nothing else.

Small but real

  1. The resume page is broken on mobile and serves two different resumes. The 800px PDF iframe renders as a dead grey rectangle on phones. And the file it embeds is not the file the link hands out.

    files/mkonczal-resume.pdf is 108,791 bytes (md5 8393…). The GitHub copy behind "here to get my resume" is 112,774 bytes (md5 e895…). Also: "click here" as link text.

  2. There is no way to buy the book. The only link in the whole band is the publisher's name.

  3. The sidebar email is a personal Gmail. You are inviting press and editors to konczal@gmail.com.

  4. Portfolio defaults to 5 rows of 66, across 14 pages, and leaks "(filtered from 511)" to the reader. Default to 25 and drop the parenthetical. That string is DataTables talking to itself.

  5. --mk-copper now holds a blue. #3067a8. Your own comment admits it. Rename it before it bites you in six months.

  6. Two target="_blank" links are missing rel="noopener".

Deeper changes

These are judgment calls, not defects. Each one is a bet about what the site is for.

1. The homepage has no thesis

The first screen is a low-resolution TV screengrab plus three paragraphs of third-person CV prose. On mobile the photo alone eats 600 of 812 pixels before a single word appears. Nowhere above the fold does the site say what you think.

You have a thesis and it is a good one: the cost of living is the defining economic problem of the moment, and fixing it means confronting both broken markets and broken incomes. It is in the Affordability Framework blurb, buried in section three. Put it in the hero, in your voice, at display size, and demote the bio to a short credentials line underneath. The page currently opens with your resume when it should open with your argument.

Related: replace the photo. It is a cable-hit screengrab, upscaled, with a green-screen DC skyline behind you. Every other asset here is deliberate. That one reads as whatever was on the camera roll.

2. The macro strip is your signature and it does not say anything

Eight numbers, eight sparklines, zero interpretation. For someone whose entire brand is "here is what this number actually means," that is the deepest miss on the site. Give each cell a one-line read in your voice: "V/U at 1.04: the labor market is no longer tight." Then the strip stops being decoration and becomes the reason people bookmark the page.

It also has no source note and no as-of date. That is the one thing here a peer would actually flag, and it cuts against your own rule about recording vintage timestamps. Add "Source: FRED. Data through July 2026. Updated [render date]." with a link to the code.

And it is badly placed on mobile. Eight full-width cells stacked means roughly 3,600px of dashboard before a reader reaches anything you wrote. Below 760px, show three cells and a "see all indicators" link.

3. Decide what this site is for, relative to the Substack

Right now the two compete for the same job and the Substack wins on every axis: it has the posts, the audience, the subscribe flow, the comments. So stop treating this as a second publication and make it the front door to the first one. Latest becomes the feed. Portfolio becomes the permanent archive that Substack cannot be, holding the 346 press hits, the testimony, the books. Start Here becomes the on-ramp. Every path ends at subscribe.

4. There is no press kit, and it is costing you bookings

346 press hits and congressional testimony, and a line in the Portfolio blurb saying "feel free to contact me if you'd like me to write for you or join you on air" with no email next to it. A producer on deadline needs four things in one place: a downloadable headshot, a 40-word bio they can paste, a topics list (inflation, affordability, housing, Fed, tax), and a professional email. Small page, high conversion.

5. Everything lives on one URL, so nothing can circulate

index.qmd is 38 KB of source producing 276 KB of HTML carrying six sections, and about.html, portfolio.html, writings.html, clips.html, and freedom_from_the_market.html are all JavaScript redirect stubs pointing back at it. No permalink to the book page, no per-section social card, no way for one argument to travel on its own. Give at least Book, Portfolio, and Press real pages.

6. Good type system, under-used, and the band rhythm is broken

Every section carries 21.25px of top and bottom padding, so sections butt into each other instead of reading as separate movements. Worse, the alternation actually fails: #start-here and #portfolio are both #fbfcfc, adjacent, with 42px between them, so there is no perceived boundary at all. Go to 4 or 5rem of band padding and fix the alternation.

The measure is also out of control. Capping prose is free and makes the whole page easier to read.

Computed: #start-here p renders at 938px at 18px, about 108 characters per line. Your hero is correctly set at about 62. This document is capped at 68.

The larger typographic point: Newsreader is doing one job at one size. Body is 18px, H2 is 33px, and that is the entire scale. No display voice, no tension between the serif and Inter, nothing memorable in the type itself. You have a characterful face and you are using it as a delivery vehicle.

7. The framework is fighting you

Bootstrap (497 KB raw), jQuery (89 KB), DataTables, crosstalk, and tippy, for a site that uses close to none of it. That stack is also what produces the Portfolio experience you are stuck with: DataTables is why the default is 5 rows and why "(filtered from 511)" leaks out. A hand-rolled filter and sort over a JSON file would be about 60 lines, fully yours to style, and would drop most of that weight.

If you only do five things

The short list

In this order. The first one matters most, and it is the only one on the list that keeps paying after you do it.

  1. Pull the Substack feed into "Latest." Fixes staleness at the root, permanently
  2. Add an inline subscribe block after "Start Here." About 30 minutes, directly serves the 9,000 goal
  3. Add description: to index.qmd, plus a 1200 x 630 social card. About 20 minutes
  4. Resize the four images and add loading="lazy". About 20 minutes, saves roughly 1.4 MB
  5. Put a one-line read under each macro indicator, plus a source and vintage note. Turns your best asset into your signature
