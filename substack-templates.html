Four ways to place the Substack band

Each option below is built with the site's real tokens (petrol/chart-blue/copper, Newsreader + Inter, the same band padding and accent-rule treatment as styles.css), so it reads exactly as it would sitting on the homepage — right after Start Here, right before Latest — rather than as an abstract comp.

The pitch copy and post titles in each one are placeholders in a plausible voice, not your actual words or actual posts. Pick a shape first; we write the real copy once you know which direction you want.

Option 1

The straight ask

A full band, same weight as the Book band, dark petrol with the gridline motif. Inline email field, no separate click-through. Best if: you want the newsletter treated as a first-class section, not an afterthought. Cost: the biggest visual commitment of the four — it's a whole "moment," same as the book.

Option 2

The preview feed

Same list grammar as "Latest," branded as the newsletter, showing three real post titles before the ask — proof before the pitch. Best if: you think skeptics convert better after seeing the actual writing. Cost: it's a second list right before the real "Latest" list — worth checking that it doesn't feel redundant back to back.

Option 3

Split editorial

Two columns, like the hero: a real pitch statement at display size on the left, a bordered widget card on the right that looks native rather than like a raw embedded iframe. Best if: you want the newsletter to feel like a proper publication, not a form. Cost: the tallest of the four, and the widget card is the most custom-built piece.

Compact banner

One thin bar, one line, no band of its own — sits directly under Start Here without a full section's worth of padding. Best if: you don't want a fifth "moment" competing with the book and the macro strip. Cost: lowest visual weight, probably the lowest conversion of the four.
